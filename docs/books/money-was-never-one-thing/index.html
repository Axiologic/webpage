The myth of one money

Money has long been a layered arrangement of credit, commodities, public authority, private promises, accounting, and trust. The book recovers that plurality before asking what has been gained—and hidden—by modern monetary singleness.

A grammar for monetary design

Alternative currencies are not one policy. Their unit, issuer, redemption, circulation, governance, and relation to taxes and debt determine whether they create useful coordination, exclusion, instability, or new dependencies.

Money is not a thing found in the world. It is an agreement whose architecture decides who can act.

Experiments with boundaries

Historical experiments, mutual credit, local systems, cryptocurrencies, and programmable money become cases for institutional judgment rather than slogans for or against monetary innovation.

For whom

Readers of political economy, economic history, institutions, and monetary design.
