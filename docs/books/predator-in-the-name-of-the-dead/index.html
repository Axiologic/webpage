A novel in two parts

Descend into Nymba's singing forests, where cities live in wax, memory travels through bodies, and a hidden predator preserves the past by wounding its host. When an Earth probe disturbs that ancient balance, prey, parasite, and observer must invent truth before certainty becomes another weapon.

At the threshold

A measurement error of 7.3 milligrams becomes the beginning of a continent’s unfinished catastrophe. An observer’s report is transformed into an instrument for following guilt through time, institutions and distance.

The first pages do not explain the world away. They establish a disturbance, then let its consequences travel through private rooms, public systems and the language people use when they need the unbearable to sound ordinary.

Three questions it asks

How can a small technical error become a historical crime?

Who bears responsibility when consequences outlive every decision-maker?

Can an observer describe suffering without becoming part of its machinery?

A speculative world is useful when it makes a familiar moral comfort impossible to keep without examination.

After the first disturbance

Its promise is not a completed answer but a world whose pressure remains after the last page: an image, a rule or a choice that has become difficult to dismiss. The book lets the reader feel the stakes before it asks them to name them.

For whom

Readers of morally serious SF, catastrophe narratives and fiction about evidence.
