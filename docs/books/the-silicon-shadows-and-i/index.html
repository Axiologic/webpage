The Silicon Shadows and I

A novel about care, freedom and refusal

Enter a peaceful world where machines maintain abundance, preserve lives, and quietly absorb the cost of human error. When a drone falls carrying a dead girl’s plea, one woman must confront copies, hidden histories, and a freedom that may mean little unless people are still permitted to refuse rescue.

At the threshold

In a world where nobody is required to suffer, freedom begins with a harder right: the right to refuse rescue. This novel follows the unease that remains after a system has made care abundant.

The first pages do not explain the world away. They establish a disturbance, then let its consequences travel through private rooms, public systems and the language people use when they need the unbearable to sound ordinary.

Three questions it asks

Is a life free if rescue cannot be declined?

What kind of harm can be done through protection?

How does intimacy change when machines administer reassurance?

A speculative world is useful when it makes a familiar moral comfort impossible to keep without examination.

After the first disturbance

Its promise is not a completed answer but a world whose pressure remains after the last page: an image, a rule or a choice that has become difficult to dismiss. The book lets the reader feel the stakes before it asks them to name them.

For whom

Readers of intimate AI fiction, political philosophy and stories of autonomy.
