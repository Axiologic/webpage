Even Though It Is Run by People Who Don’t Understand It

A realistic defense of survival: neither denial nor apocalypse, but a search for the capacities that let complex societies perceive danger, coordinate action, and revise themselves in time.

The danger is real; fatalism is not

The book takes climate, war, biosecurity, technological concentration, and institutional failure seriously. It rejects the comforting claim that progress automatically protects us, but also the idea that the future is already lost.

Building a cockpit for civilization

Modern systems create consequences faster than public institutions can understand them. The practical response is better sensing, resilient infrastructure, distributed competence, accountable authority, and a culture that treats correction as strength.

Hope is not a prediction. It is the work of keeping the path of correction open.

A grown-up species

The argument closes with the political and moral disciplines required to survive abundance and power: preserve disagreement, prepare for shocks, and make every large system answerable to the people who live inside it.

For whom

Readers of resilience, long-term governance, technology, and civilizational risk.
