1.1 The Discovery Flood and the Permission Paradox

The permission paradox predates AI. The farther an idea lies from the present, the fewer of the present's credibility signals it can possess. It lacks users because the product does not exist, citations because the field has not formed, benchmarks because the phenomenon is not yet measured, and prestigious sponsors because prestige was earned in the old paradigm. The idea needs resources to create evidence but needs evidence to receive resources. AI adds a second paradox: it makes unfamiliar ideas easier to describe and harder to distinguish. When every proposal can sound complete, reviewers may rely even more on reputation, social proof, inherited categories, and standardized scores.

3.1 When Novelty Outruns Absorption

Google's AI co-scientist illustrates the direction. Its architecture uses specialized roles for generation, reflection, ranking, evolution, proximity analysis, and meta-review, coordinated around a research objective. The system generates candidate hypotheses, subjects them to iterative criticism and competition, and can incorporate tools and expert feedback. Google's Empirical Research Assistance system addresses a different bottleneck by generating and optimizing executable scientific software against empirical criteria. FutureHouse's Robin workflow coordinates literature and analysis agents with preclinical validation. ARIA is funding projects that connect AI scientists to automated laboratories and describes the resulting transition in unusually direct terms: hypothesis generation is becoming abundant, while the capacity to test ideas in the physical world is becoming the bottleneck.

The immediate consequence is a novelty-absorption gap. In software-dominant fields, some candidate ideas can be compiled, tested, benchmarked, and rejected quickly. In chemistry, materials science, medicine, ecology, and engineering, reality has a slower clock. Instruments, samples, approvals, manufacturing, longitudinal outcomes, and replications cannot be generated merely by increasing inference compute. Even where experiments are computational, a benchmark can be optimized without representing the underlying scientific objective. The Discovery Flood therefore does not consist of discoveries in the strict sense. It consists of an expanding mixture of conjectures, partial results, designs, arguments, prototypes, and real breakthroughs competing for a validation capacity that does not scale at the same rate.

The flood metaphor is more useful than the image of an AI assistant because it describes a change in the environment, not merely a faster worker inside the old workflow. Many sources can feed the flow continuously; the level can rise gradually until routines that once seemed adequate become bottlenecks; and the institutional failure comes from a mismatch between inflow and absorption capacity. The metaphor also preserves the positive side of the phenomenon. Water can destroy, but it can also irrigate, connect, and create new productive terrain. The objective is not to stop the flow. It is to build channels through which valuable novelty can move, reservoirs in which uncertain ideas can be tested without demanding immediate adoption, and measurement systems that reveal where attention is most valuable.

Executable science is one name for an infrastructure built around this shift. It does not imply that all science can be reduced to software or formal proof. It means that as much of the research object as possible becomes inspectable and actionable by machines: claims linked to source spans, data linked to transformations, methods linked to code and protocols, conclusions linked to uncertainty, and proposed impact linked to explicit value assumptions. The objective is not a paper that can be read by a computer. It is a research process in which computers can help locate the exact boundary between what has been shown and what is merely being said.

5.1 Users, Decisions, and the Longview Dossier

Grant reviewers are the clearest initial use case because the decision has an explicit source document, an explicit rubric, a declared funding context, and an observable downstream outcome. Review is also under measurable pressure. The rapid rise in submissions increases workload, while studies of peer review continue to find substantial reviewer disagreement. Pier and colleagues found very low agreement when the same NIH applications were evaluated by different reviewer groups. Bol and colleagues showed how small early differences in funding can compound into much larger later funding advantages. These findings do not make peer review irrational; they show why a decision-support layer should expose uncertainty rather than convert it into false precision.

For a grant reviewer, Longview would run beside the authorised review process. It could reconstruct the proposal without author identity during an idea-first stage, retrieve a portfolio of supporting and contradicting evidence, identify whether the claimed novelty is mechanistic or mainly rhetorical, and produce several conditional success worlds. It would then ask which assumptions dominate the upside and which small experiment would most efficiently discriminate among them. The reviewer would see the result as a second opinion and remain responsible for the decision. In regimes where funder rules prohibit AI-assisted merit assessment, the technology must not be used for that purpose. The European Research Council, for example, clarified in March 2026 that reviewers may not use AI to summarise proposals, assess scientific merit, or generate draft evaluations. That constraint is not a footnote; secure and authorised deployment is part of the research problem.

6.1 An LLM-Heavy Architecture

The third component is a panel of genuinely differentiated evaluators. A scientific-validity agent examines mechanisms and evidence. An implementation agent asks what would be required to build and operate the system. A systems agent examines dependencies and bottlenecks. An adoption agent studies incentives, workflow change, and diffusion. An institutional agent asks which organizational forms could carry the idea. A security and misuse agent explores adversarial paths. An axiological agent identifies value conflicts and affected groups. A historical analogist examines patterns without treating analogy as proof.

6.2 A Neuro-Symbolic Architecture and an Executable Review Stack

The symbolic kernel applies several families of operations. Argumentation operators propagate support and attack while preserving cycles and unresolved conflicts. Constraint operators test whether a scenario requires mutually incompatible assumptions or unavailable resources. Causal operators examine interventions and counterfactuals. Backcasting starts from a high-impact outcome and identifies necessary intermediate states. Morphological operators combine alternative values of key variables to generate structurally different scenarios. Multi-criteria operators compare implications under explicit value weights without claiming that the weights are objective facts.

7.2 Reputation Firewalls, Qualified Lotteries, and Portfolio Design

This is not an abstract provocation. New Zealand's Health Research Council uses anonymous assessment and random ordering for eligible Explorer Grant proposals judged transformative and viable. The Swiss National Science Foundation allows lots when its funding line cuts through proposals of indistinguishable scientific quality. The British Academy has extended partial randomization of its Small Research Grants to 2028 after reporting a broader pool of applicants, awardees, and represented institutions. Austria's FWF concluded in 2026 that its 1000 Ideas program is strongest as a high-risk, high-gain format with small budgets, short terms, anonymous assessment, and the possibility of partial randomization.

8.2 Capital That Buys Learning

Modern frontier capital is more diverse. Some mechanisms fund the individual. Emergent Ventures has supported unusual projects through relatively open applications. Astera's residency provides time, salary, and research budgets for public-good work before it is clear whether the result should become a company, nonprofit, standard, or open artifact. Foresight Institute's communities and funding programs cultivate technical work at the edge of AI, science, longevity, and related domains. Encode, developed by Pillar VC with ARIA in the United Kingdom, offers an AI-for-science pathway in which researchers can develop ideas before choosing between venture, open source, and other institutional forms. Fifty Years' 5050 program and Activate's fellowships help scientists cross the difficult transition from research to company creation without making the first support conditional on a polished venture persona.

Venture funds occupy a different point in the stack. Breakout Ventures focuses on science-driven companies and emphasizes the ability to recognize inflection points before markets do. Fifty Years invests in companies aimed at large human and planetary needs. Lux Capital and DCVC have long backed deep-technology companies whose markets require scientific and technical conviction. Khosla Ventures explicitly describes seed investment as support for science or business experiments, including ideas with substantial probability of failure, provided the key technical risk and economic upside can be understood. These organizations are not interchangeable, and none is an automatic fit for a particular project. They are useful starting points because their mandates acknowledge category risk.

9.2 A Research Programme and European Research Use Case

The European context is unusually favourable to testing this idea, although the book does not claim automatic eligibility for any call. The European Commission's AI in Science Strategy treats AI as a technology that can reshape scientific work from literature review to laboratory automation and has created RAISE, the Resource for AI Science in Europe, as a virtual institute pooling compute, data, talent, and funding. The 2026-2027 Horizon Europe work programme includes a horizontal AI in Science call. The 2027 call HORIZON-RAISE-2027-01 is scheduled to open on 22 September 2026 with a deadline of 2 February 2027; its Scientific Discovery topic has an indicative budget of EUR 29 million and expects projects around EUR 10 million. Longview's relevance is not that it alone answers that topic, but that AI-generated scientific discovery creates exactly the validation and prioritisation problem the technology is designed to study.
