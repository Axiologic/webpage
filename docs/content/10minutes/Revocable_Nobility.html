Two Philosophies and One Contradiction

Metacult theory told me that good ideas do not win because they are good. They win when people carry them, finance them, repeat them, teach them to children, protect them through bad years, reward those who serve them, and build institutions able to act in their name. It treats collective identity not as an embarrassing residue of primitive life but as a social technology. Story, ritual, reputation, membership, treasury, memory, and sacrifice can make a group capable of work no individual could sustain. The same machinery can create a school, a cooperative, a scientific society, a religion, a liberation movement, a company, or a prison for the mind. The metacult is the attempt to use that machinery consciously while limiting leaders, doctrine, extraction, secrecy, and the right of the whole to consume the person [ALBOAIE-METACULT-2026].

The metacult needs concentrated agency. Somebody must preserve the archive, control the treasury, represent the community, make decisions under time pressure, and accept responsibility for consequences that cannot be divided into a thousand equal fragments. Outfinitism distrusts the silent expansion of every such mandate. The person competent to maintain a grid is not therefore competent to define the moral value of every use of electricity. The founder who correctly names one historical problem does not acquire a general license to govern intimacy, science, law, or the future. The expert who knows a true fact still has not proved that the fact is relevant, that the model is adequate to the whole decision, or that the expert possesses legitimate authority over those who bear the error.
