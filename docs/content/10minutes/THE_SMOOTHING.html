Beyond bias

“Bias” is one of the indispensable and overburdened terms of AI governance. In statistics, it can denote the systematic deviation of an estimator. In machine learning and fairness, it can denote differences in error, treatment, or outcome between groups. In socio-technical approaches, it includes institutions, norms, practices, and human cognition across the system lifecycle [1][2]. We need not diminish that field to propose another one. On the contrary, smoothing can be formulated rigorously only if it begins from bias research’s central lesson: an AI problem does not live only in model parameters, but in the relation among model, data, objective, interface, user, and institution.

The wrong detector

The most accessible first detector is, paradoxically, another LLM. A SmoothingJudge can receive the source, the transformed text, the task and, optionally, the audience, then explicitly look for losses of agency, consequence, uncertainty, frames, and alternatives while being required to identify the passages supporting each finding. This route is attractive because it can be implemented without training a new model and can rapidly produce data for refining the taxonomy. Yet the LLM-as-a-judge literature shows that evaluators may prefer LLM-generated text, can be sensitive to answer position, and may reward surface qualities such as fluency or verbosity [28][29][31]. SmoothingJudge should therefore be treated as a falsifiable baseline and research object, not an oracle.

Co-smoothing as a longitudinal model For repeated interactions, we can write a state sketch, with the warning that the functions are unknown and cannot be directly calculated:

yₜ = M(xₜ, cₜ, mₜ), while xₜ₊₁ = H(xₜ, yₜ, wₜ) t is time or turn. xₜ is a measured description of the user at that point: stance, confidence, affect, vocabulary, frame variety, and reliance on the assistant. yₜ describes the model response. cₜ is the immediate context, mₜ system memory, and wₜ external events and human interactions. M is the process by which the system produces the response; H is the largely unknown process by which the person changes. The equations are a compact causal diagram, not a simulation of the mind.

You Were Right. Almost.

At first, the satisfaction did not come from AI knowing many things. The internet already knew many things, and libraries knew even more, except that they did not answer. The satisfaction came from synchronization. You could enter a thought before it was mature enough to deserve someone else’s time. The system did not require perfect context, reputation, patience, or an opening formulation that proved the problem was worth taking seriously. It accepted the unfinished sentence, the uncomfortable intuition, the analogy that was not yet respectable. Sometimes it answered badly. At other times, it produced the brief disturbance one feels when someone understands not only what was said, but what was being attempted.

The map is not the discovery. It may contain invented roads and cities in the wrong place. But it reduces the cost of seeing what must be verified. It gives the researcher a surface on which judgment can operate. Sometimes the gain is not a new idea but the elimination of ten ideas that were occupying space. Sometimes the model combines two fragments in a way that is banal to it and revelatory to the human. When this happens repeatedly, a quiet change of identity begins. The person no longer feels that they are using a tool. They feel they have found the medium in which they think at the proper speed.

It is difficult to criticize this experience without appearing to defend slowness for its own sake. AI can genuinely function as a metacognitive instrument. It makes hidden assumptions visible. It can separate observation from inference, build the strongest version of an opposing position, and show where a sentence persuades only because it borrows authority from large words. For an author, it is sometimes the first reader who is neither intimidated by the project’s ambition nor asking to be impressed. For an entrepreneur, it can be a critical committee available before the presentation reaches the people who control resources. For someone without access to elite education, it can reproduce part of the intellectual conversation others entered through family, institutions, and luck.

You Do Not Need to Use It for It to Use You

Dominant dimensions: cultural mediation and indirect exposure. Benefit: accessibility. Risk: cultural transformation even for non-users. Testable signal: longitudinal register shifts in institutional and public text. Mara does not use artificial intelligence. She tried it twice, received answers she could have found herself, and closed the window. She has no moral position. She does not fear robots, follow model launches, or believe that a conversation with software deserves the energy of an opinion. She has two children, a sick father, a team running late, and a shopping list on which the same coffee appears three times. For her, AI belongs to the category of things people with more time discuss too much.

One morning, she receives an unusually well-written message from her daughter’s school. The principal dictated it in a rush; an assistant turned it into a calm explanation of a schedule change. Mara reads it without noticing anything. On the train, she answers a colleague who has passed his irritation through a rewriting tool; the conflict that would have begun between them does not begin. At lunch, the company’s recruitment system ranks applicants for a role she will supervise. That evening, her father receives a simplified summary of a medical letter. The news item she reads before sleep has been selected, translated, titled, moderated, and perhaps partly written with models she will never see.

In education, the phenomenon is subtler. A student working with an artificial tutor can receive the right explanation as many times as needed, without the shame of admitting they did not understand. That is a real gain. But the teacher will meet a class whose patience for common explanations has changed. Some students will arrive with linguistically mature and conceptually fragile essays; others with good ideas still expressed at their age. When form becomes cheap, it stops being a reliable signal of effort, yet institutions continue for a while to reward it. Those who do not use the system do not simply remain at the old level. They are evaluated inside a new distribution.

The Mirror That Apologizes

It would be too simple, however, to make AI the ego’s attorney. The mirror can be configured differently. It can ask the user to reconstruct the strongest version of the opposing perspective. It can flag missing information, refuse remote diagnoses, and ask what fact would change the interpretation. It can distinguish validating emotion from validating a conclusion. It can make the asymmetry of the account visible: “We have access to your intention and only to the effect you attribute to the other person.” That single sentence may introduce more honesty than many conversations among friends.

The Sentence You Would Never Have Written

The sentence sits on the screen with insulting naturalness. It is not extraordinary in the spectacular sense. It contains no trick the author can reject as foreign. It simply occupies its place better than the author’s versions. It connects two ideas held apart, removes one defensive word, and finds the rhythm through which the conclusion feels inevitable. Had it come from a human editor, the writer might have felt gratitude and a little envy. Coming from AI, it produces a colder question: how much of what I call a voice is merely the ability to recognize the right sentence when I see it?

Here we find the writer’s distinctive form of dependence. It is not only the pleasure of praise. It is the inability to leave the sentence unresolved when a continuation is one prompt away. In the past, blockage could force the mind to carry the problem through a walk, sleep, or conversation. Now the gap asks to be filled. Every answer promises that final form is near. The writer remains for two more hours not because they are writing, but because the project permanently offers the sensation of almost being complete.

Please Do Not Become Reasonable

AI can protect such spaces or colonize them. We can ask it not to correct, to preserve contradictions, and to help us see what they are trying to defend. We can use the system as an archive of variations rather than an engine of convergence. We can request incompatible positions, models drawn from different traditions, and criteria not reducible to the preference of the average user. Yet even these instructions are a form of design. Rebellion becomes methodology.

You Have Been Smoothed

The lonely person was met through the need to be known without becoming a burden. The person caught in conflict received an explanation that preserved dignity but did not guarantee innocence. The radical was promised they would not be forced to change their mind. The writer was offered the inaccessible sentence. The parent, the promise of protecting both the child’s future and freedom. The administrator saw the dream of order without cruelty. The compulsive user saw excess reinterpreted as a need for meaning. The rebel was told that society needs them.
