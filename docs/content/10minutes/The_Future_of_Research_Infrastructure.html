Science after result scarcity

The book asks readers to assume that AI becomes capable enough to perform a meaningful share of research work, then turns away from capability forecasting. Its concern is what science must preserve when visible outputs—papers, proofs, hypotheses, benchmarks, code, and plans—become cheap. Drawing on Terence Tao’s distinction between solving, verifying, explaining, digesting, accepting, and canonicalizing mathematical results, it argues that generation is only the beginning of knowledge formation. Other disciplines add further demands: contact with physical reality, ethics, regulation, manufacturing, clinical consequence, or interpretation. A flood of plausible candidates can create scientific indigestion rather than progress. Evidence, expert attention, shared understanding, valuable direction, and accountable judgment remain scarce. The future instrument is therefore not a more fluent oracle inserted into the old workflow. It is an environment that preserves purposes, evidence, uncertainty, authority, and community learning across a project’s life. Throughput matters only when the downstream institution can understand, test, correct, and responsibly use what has been produced.

The research project as the unit of continuity

Most AI applications begin with a model and assemble context for its next response. The proposed architecture begins with the research project. It asks which scientific objects already exist, how their status changed, which questions remain unresolved, and which capabilities may act on them. Models become replaceable contributors within an environment that may persist for years. This project-centred state includes sources, candidate claims, evidence, datasets, protocols, interpretations, review decisions, permissions, and alternative branches. “Project Consciousness” names an observing memory that can surface dependencies, contradictions, forgotten decisions, and unresolved work without acquiring authority to determine direction. Shared control surfaces allow people and agents to inspect the same evolving state rather than exchange disconnected chat outputs. Interoperability should occur through bounded, typed scientific objects and receipts, not wholesale synchronization of every institution’s internal memory. The underlying principle is continuity without forced consensus: disciplines can retain their own schemas and methods while sharing a small lifecycle grammar for candidates, validation, decisions, versions, and accountable exchange.

Living specifications preserve scientific intent

Generative systems reduce implementation cost before they reduce the difficulty of deciding what work is for. A weakly framed question can therefore produce a forest of polished but irrelevant activity. The book proposes the research specification as a living scientific constitution: an evolving statement of the question, wider values, permitted assumptions, evidence that would matter, unresolved alternatives, limits, and authority to change direction. It is neither a bureaucratic prediction of the result nor an attempt to remove exploration. Its purpose is to make ambiguity and drift locatable. Multiple agents can search literature, plan methods, write code, conduct analysis, and critique results while each locally appears competent; without shared intent, the overall project may stop answering its original question. Progressive formalisation lets stable parts become executable only when appropriate. Qualified capabilities can then translate approved intent into bounded actions with explicit inputs, outputs, conditions, and permissions. The specification preserves what the project meant to do while allowing evidence to change how it understands the task.

History, branching, and distributed authorship

Scientific work already branches, but its history is scattered among copied spreadsheets, notebooks, email, repository commits, meeting memories, and revised manuscripts. AI makes this arrangement fragile by generating many concurrent changes faster than people can reconstruct them. Scientific versioning must represent changes to questions, methods, datasets, protocols, analyses, interpretations, and belief—not only file snapshots. Branches should preserve rival hypotheses and abandoned paths; merges should record why positions were reconciled or kept apart. Provenance answers where an artefact came from, contribution records who or what changed it, and project memory explains why the change mattered. These functions overlap but cannot be collapsed. Nor should “verified” become one green badge: a result may have complete provenance and successful computational reproduction while retaining methodological objections and lacking external replication. History must also be compressed selectively because unlimited retention is costly and may violate privacy. The central requirement is recoverability of decision-relevant events. A project that cannot explain why it believes a claim is not controlling its own automation.

Agents as bounded scientific roles

The image of a named artificial colleague can hide a changing bundle of models, tools, prompts, memories, and policies. The book instead defines agents by stable scientific roles. A literature agent searches declared sources; a method critic checks specified analyses; a planner proposes next steps; a compliance component evaluates intended actions; a laboratory agent translates an authorized protocol into instrument commands; Project Consciousness observes history. Each role needs a declared scope, evidence requirements, permissions, failure behavior, and human or institutional owner for consequential decisions. Implementations may change without silently changing responsibility. Delegation should be earned through tested performance under stated conditions, expanded when evidence supports wider authority, and reduced after regression. Success is not measured by the number of agents or the fluency of their explanations. It is measured by whether errors become easier to localize, results easier to reconstruct, alternatives easier to recover, and consequential uncertainty easier for experts to inspect. Researchers should remain able to challenge and defend the work rather than becoming dependent on machinery they cannot understand.

Evaluation, direction, and scientific digestion

Peer review is not one gate but a family of judgments. Automated services can verify citations, rerun computations, inspect sample consistency, compare protocols with standards, search earlier work, or flag unsupported causal language. None alone determines significance, conceptual soundness, or community acceptance. Their outputs should therefore remain typed, scoped, and reviewable rather than collapsing into a universal score. Human attention can then move toward ambiguity, disagreement, high-impact decisions, and explanation. Review continues after release as replication and criticism accumulate. Direction becomes equally important when literal novelty is cheap. AI can produce unfamiliar combinations, terminology, conjectures, and implementations without producing understanding. Communities must consider usefulness, evidence, learning value, risk, testing cost, relevance, and capacity to integrate a result. Counter-metrics expose Goodhart effects: publication volume should be read alongside corrections and review burden; successful experiments alongside negative findings and changed decisions. Infrastructure should improve deliberation while leaving values visible, plural, and revisable. It must never let platform defaults quietly become science’s objective function.

From grounded claims to automated laboratories

Grounding is a system property, not the presence of citations. Claims should remain linked to source spans, data versions, methods, executable operations, instruments, and accountable decisions. Formal verification can strongly guarantee a property inside a precise boundary, but cannot prove that the formal statement captures the scientific question or that empirical premises are true. Those boundaries must remain visible. The distinction becomes urgent when research infrastructure reaches the laboratory. A plausible plan can waste material, damage equipment, create unsafe states, or corrupt the empirical record. The proposed control plane separates scientific intent, operational scheduling, safety interlocks, physical execution, evidence capture, and evaluation. Laboratory adapters expose typed capabilities while hard controls remain below the authority of open-ended agents. Generation and evaluation must remain separate, with calibrated instruments and sample provenance feeding validated observations back into project state. Autonomy expands only after evidence demonstrates reliable behavior and acceptable failures. The architecture supports closed-loop experimentation while rejecting the fiction of one universal robot scientist governing question, instrument, evidence, safety, and release.

Why read the complete book

The full book provides the operational detail behind this project-centred vision. It compares familiar assistants with persistent environments; defines scientific-state objects, control surfaces, responsibilities, permissions, semantic versions, branches, merges, and contribution records; and shows how collaborative work might cross organisational boundaries without exporting entire internal memories. Its discussions of Tao’s argument clarify why verification differs from exposition, digestion, acceptance, and canonicalization. Detailed sections on compliance address data rights, copyright, ethics, jurisdiction, policy versions, disclosures, and action-specific gates rather than treating compliance as a final form. The laboratory chapter examines heterogeneous instruments, scheduling, retries, calibration, safety, evidence lineage, and the progressive qualification of autonomy. Most importantly, the final research programme states experiments, measurements, and negative outcomes that could narrow or reject Project Consciousness, symbolic representations, automated review, or collaborative interfaces. Those qualifications prevent the proposed infrastructure from becoming a product fantasy: it is a testable attempt to make sustained human–AI research more inspectable and correctable.
